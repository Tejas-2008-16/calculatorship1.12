How Gratuity Is Calculated: The Exact Statutory Formula

The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 establishes a statutory retirement benefit designed to reward employees for continuous service. For employees covered by the Act, the law treats each working month as containing 26 working days (excluding 4 Sundays), and provides 15 days of wages for every completed year:

Gratuity = (Last Drawn Monthly Basic + DA) × (15 / 26) × Completed Years of Service

Worked Example (Illustration Only)

Suppose an employee draws a last monthly Basic Salary of ₹35,000 and Dearness Allowance of ₹5,000 (total ₹40,000) and separates after 8 years and 7 months of continuous service:

  • Because the additional service (7 months) exceeds 6 months, the tenure rounds up to 9 years.
  • 15 days of wages = ₹40,000 × (15 / 26) = ₹23,076.92
  • Total Gratuity = ₹23,076.92 × 9 = ₹2,07,692.
  • Because ₹2,07,692 is below the ₹20,00,000 exemption cap, the entire amount is 100% tax-free.

Tax Exemption Rules Under Section 10(10)

Under the Income-tax Act, gratuity received upon retirement or separation is exempt up to the least of three amounts:

  1. The actual gratuity amount received.
  2. The statutory formula amount: (15 / 26) × Last Drawn Wages × Tenure.
  3. The statutory monetary limit of ₹20,00,000 (₹20 Lakh).

For Central and State Government employees, gratuity is 100% tax-exempt without monetary limits under Section 10(10)(i).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 5 years of service mandatory to receive gratuity?

For permanent employees, 5 years of continuous service is mandatory under Section 4(1) of the Act. However, this condition is waived in the event of the employee's death or disablement due to accident or disease, or for fixed-term contract workers completing at least one year.

Can an employer pay more than ₹20 Lakh gratuity?

Yes. An employer can pay an ex-gratia amount higher than ₹20 Lakh based on contract or executive policy. However, the excess amount above ₹20 Lakh is subject to income tax at your applicable slab rates.
